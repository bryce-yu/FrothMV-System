# Dataset Summary

## Raw dataset location

The full Kaggle froth-bubble segmentation dataset is stored locally outside the FrothMV-System repository and outside OneDrive.

The location is configured through the `FROTH_DATASET_ROOT` environment variable.

## Folder structure

The dataset contains three predefined splits:

- `train/train_images`
- `train/train_masks`
- `valid/valid_images`
- `valid/valid_masks`
- `test/test_images`
- `test/test_masks`

All images and masks are stored as PNG files.

## Visual mask review

A sample of image-mask pairs was inspected from the train (5), valid (3) and test (3) splits using 'src/visualise_dataset_samples.py'.

The review displayed:

- the original froth image
- the supplied ground-truth mask
- contours extracted from the mask overlaid on the original image

### General observations

- As was noted in the Project 1 Milestone Report, although the outlines do not perfectly follow the rounded shape of every bubble they do show the majority of the area covered by each bubble.
- Image and mask dimensions were consistent for the inspected samples.
- Contours provide usable approximate bubble boundaries within the froth surface.
- Some neighbouring bubbles may be represented as connected regions.
- Some boundaries are irregular due to lighting, glare and unclear bubble edges
- Overall, the masks appear suitable for general bubble count and size estimation.

### Limitations observed

- In numerous cases, shadows can cover up to the whole bottom half of the tank/cell surface in the image - whilst this may not impact average bubble size estimation too much it will impact bubble count if comparing an image where the full surface is visible versus only half visible.
- As well as shadows, resolution or level of blur seems to vary depending on image, which has some impact on the accuracy of the masks. 
- In some (maybe half) of the sample images, masks suggest some bubbles exist outside the froth surface, which shouldn't be possible. Looking at the original image does not seem to be glare specifically, so likely a shortcoming/error of the segmentation mask algorithm used. But since the Kaggle authors do specify the use of YOLOv8 Segment Anything models likely will just accept this. 

### Decision

The dataset will be retained for development of the YOLOv8 segmentation and bubble-metric extraction pipeline.

## Reduced development dataset

A reproducible reduced dataset was selected from the full Kaggle dataset to support rapid local development and testing.

The selected subset contains:

- 200 training image-mask pairs
- 40 validation image-mask pairs
- 40 testing image-mask pairs

The selected files are recorded in CSV manifest files under `dataset/manifests/`.

The raw PNG files remain in the external local dataset folder and are not duplicated inside the project repository.

Selection was performed using a fixed random seed of 42. Relative paths were recorded so that the same subset can be accessed from both development laptops through `FROTH_DATASET_ROOT`.

Manual visual inspection of a range of the images selected was conducted, and confirmed sufficient visual diversity in terms of bubble size, density and both clear images and challenging glare/boundaries. 

## Ground-truth mask metric extraction

The fixed reduced manifests were used to access 200 training, 40 validation and 40 testing image-mask pairs from the external raw dataset.

Metrics were extracted directly from the supplied ground-truth masks using OpenCV contour detection. The initial image-level outputs were:

- bubble count
- mean contour area in pixels squared
- mean equivalent circular diameter in pixels
- mean circularity

Contours smaller than 10 pixels squared were excluded from the initial summary to reduce the influence of very small mask fragments.

The results were saved under `results/mask_metrics/`.

A preliminary comparison between selected images and metric values showed that:
- Images with many smaller regions tend to have higher bubble count, as expected.
- Images with visibly larger bubbles tend to have both larger mean area and equivalent diameter, as expected.
- Circularity may be more difficult to measure and use, as for all manifest images the majority return a circularity value in the 0.6 range, with just some going to 0.5 or 0.7.  
- All manifest image masks contained contours smaller than 10 pixels squared as the minimum contour area extracted from every image was exactly 10.0, which was the lower limit set. 

## Baseline YOLOv8 test inference

The previously trained YOLOv8 segmentation model was evaluated on the fixed 40-image reduced test manifest.

The initial inference configuration was:

- confidence threshold: 0.25
- inference image size: 640 pixels
- minimum contour area: 10 pixels squared

For each test image, the predicted instance masks were combined into a binary mask. Contours were then extracted using the same approach used for the ground-truth masks.

The predicted outputs included:

- YOLO instance count
- contour-based bubble count
- mean contour area
- mean equivalent circular diameter
- mean circularity

Predicted metrics were compared with the corresponding metrics extracted from the supplied ground-truth masks.

### Baseline mean absolute errors

  bubble_count: 128.4750
  mean_area_pixels: 72.2486
  mean_equivalent_diameter_pixels: 2.2904
  mean_circularity: 0.0408

### Initial observations

- Predicted masks tended to underestimate the number of bubbles in the image.
- The images with large bubble count errors are the ones with many small, dense bubbles, many of which were not picked up by the prediction mask (e.g. F5_2_2_2.ts-frames_frame-1110.png).
- The images with lower bubble-count errors generally appeared to contain moderately sized bubbles at moderate density, although this was based on qualitative inspection of the evaluated samples (e.g. F5_1_2_1.ts-frames_frame-1785.png).
- Per image percentage errors are largely in the range of 40% or less.

- Predicted masks tended to overestimate mean bubble area. A likely contributing factor is that many small bubbles were missed, removing low-area objects from the mean. Merging of neighbouring bubbles may also increase the predicted contour areas.
- As the number of missed bubbles in each image varies, the impact of these missed bubbles differs for each image in terms of mean area, as errors are in a wide range from as low as 0.32% up to 130.92%.
- However, overall, per image percentage errors are still largely in the range of 50% or less.

- The mean absolute error in mean equivalent diameter (pixels) of 2.2904 is reasonably small compared to the typical diameters in low tens of pixels, with the majority of per image percentage errors being below 20% and only a few at around 40%.

- The mean absolute error in mean circularity of 0.0408 compared to the scale of 0-1 is reasonably small, with the majority of per image percentage errors being below 10%.

### Baseline conclusion

The existing YOLOv8 segmentation model produced predicted masks for all 40 test images, demonstrating complete inference coverage on the fixed reduced test subset.

Overall, the model is suitable as an initial baseline for estimating average bubble geometry, but requires improvement before bubble count can be treated as a reliable supervisory variable, especially under dense small-bubble conditions.

## Controlled inference experiments

Three configurations were evaluated on the same fixed 40-image test manifest at an inference image size of 640 pixels.

| Configuration | Confidence | Minimum area (px²) | Bubble-count MAE | Mean-area MAE | Diameter MAE | Circularity MAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 0.25 | 10 | 128.4750 | 72.2486 | 2.2904 | 0.0408 |
| Lower confidence | 0.15 | 10 | 103.1250 | 62.4766 | 2.1010 | 0.0369 |
| Smaller contour filter | 0.25 | 5 | 128.1000 | 71.9972 | 2.2734 | 0.0404 |

### Preliminary interpretation

- Overall, changing minimum area from 10 to 5 barely changes the results, suggesting that the main issue is missing YOLO detections, and not OpenCV removing small contours afterwards - all mean absolute errors remain very similar.
- Lowering the confidence threshold did not appear to introduce substantial noisy or false regions in the inspected visualisations, and generally captured additional regions that aligned with the ground-truth masks.
- Comparing the dense, small bubbles case, the predictions and metric errors remained almost unchanged, indicating that lowering confidence alone does not address the model’s difficulty with very small, closely packed bubbles.
- Comparing the intermediate prediction case, there is a decrease in all absolute/percentage errors in metric predictions.
- Comparing the good prediction case, there is mostly an improvement in prediction - with a decrease in errors for bubble count, mean area and circularity and only an increase in error for mean diameter from about 2% to 8%. 

Overall, reducing the minimum contour area from 10 to 5 px² produced negligible changes, suggesting that the main limitation is missing YOLO detections rather than OpenCV filtering. Lowering the confidence threshold improved all four aggregate MAEs and generally improved the intermediate and good representative cases without obvious additional noise. However, it did not improve the dense small-bubble failure case. Therefore, the lower-confidence configuration is the provisional best setting, while dense small-bubble detection remains a model-level limitation.

The provisional best inference configuration was:

- confidence threshold: 0.15
- inference image size: 640 pixels
- minimum contour area: 10 pixels squared
